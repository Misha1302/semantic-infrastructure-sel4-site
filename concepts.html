Concepts

Vocabulary follows the problem.

The object model exists to preserve domain meaning across planning and enforcement. It is not a dictionary bolted onto an otherwise unchanged process model.

Subject, Class, Object

A Subject is an acting identity—human, software, or agent—with explicit authority. A Class describes semantic shape and constraints. An Object is a durable domain identity such as ServerTwin, Refrigerator#17, or Motor#4.

Property, Method, Relation, Event, StreamRef

Properties expose state, methods express finite semantic operations, relations connect objects, events record discrete occurrences, and streams represent continuous/high-rate observation channels. Treating these as distinct makes policy and execution semantics explicit.

Refrigerator#17
properties: temperature, state, powerConsumption
methods: setTemperature(value), turnOff()
streams: temperature, powerConsumption
relations: locatedIn → Room#3

AuthorityPolicy

A semantic policy can express “Alice may invoke Refrigerator#17.setTemperature only for values in [2,8]”. Some of that authority may be compiled structurally into capabilities/topology; some constraints may require a runtime guard. The exact partition is an open research question.

TemporalContract

Temporal correctness can include deadline, period, minimum inter-arrival time, latency/jitter budgets, criticality, priority/scheduling class, WCET assumptions, CPU/resource reservations, memory/allocation policy, admission constraints, and allowed blocking behaviour.

Target semantics, not measured guarantees. The repository does not yet contain a project real-time implementation or timing measurements.

Projection

A projection is a deliberately restricted view of an object or stream. Architecturally it can reduce exposed state and potentially reduce ambient authority, but concrete security/performance benefits must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

TopologyPlan and capabilities

TopologyPlan is the planned low-level realization of authorized semantic operations. seL4 capabilities are kernel authority tokens; they are not semantic policy by themselves. The planner's job is to translate between those layers without accidentally creating authority that the semantic model did not grant.
